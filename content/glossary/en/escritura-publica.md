An escritura pública is the public deed drafted and authenticated by a notario público and recorded at the Public Registry of Property. It is the instrument that proves ownership of real estate in Mexico: until a purchase reaches a registered deed, you are not an owner — you are a party to a contract.

## In practice

The distinction that costs foreign buyers the most money is this one: a private **contrato de compraventa** is a promise between two parties; the **escritura** is title, enforceable against everyone else. The private contract has its place — it reserves the lot and fixes the price and payment schedule — but it does not make you the owner and it does not protect you from a second sale of the same land.

In a Tulum land purchase the closing runs through the notaría: the [notario](/en/glossary/notario-publico) reviews the chain of title and liens on the folio real, calculates and withholds the taxes, executes the deed and files it for registration. If you are a foreign buyer, the same deed creates the [fideicomiso](/en/glossary/fideicomiso) that the [restricted zone](/en/glossary/zona-restringida) requires. Closing costs in the Riviera Maya typically land between 6% and 8% of the price — acquisition tax, registry fees, notario fees — and they vary by municipality, so confirm the exact figure with your notario before you sign.

## Watch out for

Any sale that stays in a private contract "to save on the notario," and any seller who cannot produce the folio real for what they are selling. A lot with no clear path to a deed — [ejidal](/en/glossary/ejido) land without registered dominio pleno, for example — is not a bargain: it is property you will never be able to deed, sell, or pass on cleanly.
