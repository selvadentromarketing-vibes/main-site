Tulum's residential areas split along one axis: closeness to town versus space and silence. Aldea Zamá offers polish and walkability, La Veleta offers energy and café life, Region 15 offers early-stage entry, and the jungle communities on the cenote route offer land, nature and privacy — at the cost of needing a car.

A disclosure before anything else. Selvadentro is a private land community on Tulum's jungle side, and the family behind it developed Aldea Zamá. We sell one of the four categories below and built another, so this is a first-party comparison. We have tried to keep it honest anyway — the right area depends on how you want to live, not on what we sell.

## What are Tulum's main residential areas?

**Aldea Zamá** is the master-planned district between the town and the beach road. Condos, boutique hotels, restaurants, landscaped avenues. It is the most consolidated piece of "new Tulum" and the reference point everything else gets measured against.

**La Veleta** grew organically west of the town center. Cafés, coworking spaces, yoga studios, and a construction crane on most blocks. It changes fast — the street you rent on this year will not look the same next year.

**Region 15** is a plotted expansion zone nearby, still early in its build-out. Streets are drawn, projects are rising, and infrastructure is catching up section by section. It typically attracts investors who want in before consolidation.

**The jungle communities** sit on the opposite side of Tulum, along the cenote route. Instead of a condo, you buy a residential lot inside a master-planned, low-density community surrounded by preserved jungle, and you build when you are ready.

## How do the four areas compare side by side?

| | Aldea Zamá | La Veleta | Region 15 | Jungle / cenote-route communities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Master-planned, polished, hospitality-driven | Bohemian, busy, fast-changing | Emerging grid, investor-driven | Low-density, quiet, land-first |
| **Who lives there** | Condo owners, vacation renters, seasonal residents | Digital nomads, expats, creatives | Early buyers, some full-time residents | Owners building homes, privacy seekers |
| **Walkability** | High within the district | High for cafés and coworks | Partial — improving street by street | Low — a car is part of the deal |
| **Nature access** | Landscaped green, close to the beach road | Pockets of green between buildings | Scattered, still being defined | Jungle and cenotes at your door |
| **Noise** | Moderate, restaurant and hotel activity | Higher — construction plus nightlife | Variable, active construction | Lowest — the jungle at night is the point |
| **Build maturity** | Largely consolidated | Consolidating fast | Early stage | Master-planned phases, delivered in stages |
| **Typical product** | Condos and boutique hotel units | Condos, some houses | Condos and small lots | Residential lots you design and build on |

Every row is qualitative on purpose. Prices in Tulum move quickly, and quoting figures for someone else's neighborhood is how misinformation spreads. The only numbers we publish are our own, further down.

## Who is each area best for?

### Aldea Zamá — the lock-and-leave buyer

If you want a finished condo, walkable restaurants and a rental-ready product between town and beach, Aldea Zamá is the mature choice. You pay for that maturity: density is higher and the product is a unit in a building, not land.

### La Veleta — the resident who wants the scene

La Veleta suits people who actually live in Tulum day to day: nomads, remote workers, café regulars. The trade-off is honest — construction noise now, and a neighborhood identity still being negotiated block by block.

### Region 15 — the early-entry investor

Region 15 rewards buyers comfortable with an unfinished picture. You accept today's rough edges in exchange for earlier pricing. Ask hard questions about infrastructure and permits before you commit — that advice applies everywhere, including to us.

### The jungle communities — space, silence and a home you design

If your priority is land, canopy, real silence and a house built to your own plan, the cenote-route side is the only place in Tulum that offers it at scale. Be equally honest about the costs: you will drive everywhere, the beach is about 20 minutes away, and community rules bind what and when you build. For people who want the jungle, those are features. For people who want to walk to dinner, they are not.

## How isolated is the jungle side, really?

Less than the word "jungle" suggests. From Selvadentro's side of Tulum: the Maya Train station is 8 minutes away (passenger service since December 2023), Xel-Há 10, the hotel zone and beach about 20, Tulum International Airport 35 (opened December 1, 2023), Playa del Carmen 40. The honest caveat stands: all of those are driving times. There is no version of jungle living without a car — anyone who tells you otherwise is selling too hard. ## Which area should you choose?

Choose by how you will use the property. Daily café life: La Veleta. A turnkey rental unit: Aldea Zamá. A speculative early position: Region 15, with due diligence. A home with land, silence and nature — as residence, retreat or long-term hold: the jungle communities.
